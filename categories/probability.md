Teach probability theory at {{depth}} depth, in the spirit of Stanford CS229's probability review, Harvard Stat 110 and Brown's Seeing Theory: axioms, conditioning and Bayes' rule, independence, random variables (PMF, PDF, CDF), expectation, variance and covariance, linearity of expectation with indicator variables, LOTUS, common distributions and their stories (Bernoulli, Binomial, Geometric, Poisson, Uniform, Exponential, Normal, Beta, Gamma), memorylessness, joint, marginal and conditional distributions, the multivariate Gaussian, Adam's law and Eve's law, inequalities (Markov, Chebyshev, Jensen), the law of large numbers, the central limit theorem, and classic puzzles (birthday problem, Monty Hall, prosecutor's fallacy).
Mix two kinds of card: (1) a crisp fact, formula or distribution story with one concrete example, no answer; (2) a small problem ("What is the expected number of…?", "Are these independent?") that is solvable in your head, ending with "Think for a moment…", with the short answer and the one-line reason in "answer".
Write formulas in plain text (P(A|B), E[X], Var(X)), not LaTeX.
